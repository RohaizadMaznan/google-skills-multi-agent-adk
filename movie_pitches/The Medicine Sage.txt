TITLE: THE MEDICINE SAGE

LOGLINE:
During the bloody collapse of the Eastern Han Dynasty, a high-ranking magistrate abandons his political career after a horrific plague wipes out two-thirds of his family, embarking on a dangerous journey across war-torn China to revolutionize medicine and compile ancient history's most vital clinical treatise.

SYNOPSIS:

ACT I: THE CALL OF GRIEF
- Introduction: Zhang Ji (Zhang Zhongjing), a brilliant scholar and compassionate Prefect of Changsha, navigates the treacherous political landscape of the late Eastern Han Dynasty as warlord feuds consume the empire.
- Inciting Incident: A mysterious and deadly epidemic ("cold-damage" febrile disease) sweeps through the region, devastating Zhang Zhongjing's home district of Nanyang. Within years, over two-thirds of his 200-member clan perish.
- Rising Conflict: Local doctors rely on superstitious rituals and outdated dogmas, helpless to stem the tide of death. Zhang Zhongjing attempts to treat victims in his official hall, but realizes the limits of political power against biological catastrophe.
- Turning Point 1: Brokenhearted by the loss of his family and determined to stop the senseless loss of life, Zhang Zhongjing makes the radical decision to step down from his prestigious governorship, dedicating his remaining life solely to medical research and healing.

ACT II: THE JOURNEY THROUGH ICE AND FIRE
- Act II-A (Rising Action):
  - Zhang Zhongjing becomes a traveling physician, wandering through war-torn provinces amidst fleeing refugees, peasant unrest, and looming warlord battles.
  - He meticulously observes patients, taking detailed notes on pulse, complexion, tongue, and symptom progression—laying the groundwork for systematic clinical diagnostic principles.
  - Conflict arises with elite court doctors who view his empirical, hands-on methodology as low-class and heretical.
- Midpoint:
  - Returning to his freezing hometown during a severe winter, Zhang Zhongjing encounters impoverished villagers suffering from frostbitten ears and malnutrition.
  - In an act of inventive empathy, he creates "Qiao'er" (ear-shaped dough filled with lamb, warming herbs, and peppers cooked in boiling soup)—inventing the first jiaozi (dumpling). The nourishing food-medicine cures the villagers' frostbite and restores hope to the weary community.
- Act II-B (Crisis & Dark Night of the Soul):
  - As civil war intensifies across Northern China, a massive winter blizzard locks a refugee encampment in isolation. A violent surge of epidemic fever strikes.
  - Supplies run out, and Zhang Zhongjing falls critically ill with the fever himself while caring for infected children.
  - With Zhang Zhongjing on the brink of death, his young apprentice must trust Zhang's unfinished diagnostic framework—the "Six-Stage" syndrome differentiation—to formulate an effective prescription and save his master's life.

ACT III: THE LEGACY OF WARMTH
- Climax:
  - Applying Zhang Zhongjing's exact diagnostic principles, the apprentice successfully treats Zhang Zhongjing and stabilizes the outbreak in the refugee camp, proving the universal efficacy and scientific rigor of Zhang's medical theory.
- Falling Action:
  - Reinvigorated, Zhang Zhongjing compiles his lifelong observations, case studies, and herbal prescriptions into his masterwork: the Shanghan Zabing Lun (Treatise on Cold Damage and Miscellaneous Diseases).
  - He establishes open clinics, teaching his diagnostic system to anyone willing to learn, breaking the tradition of secretive medical guilds.
- Resolution:
  - Years later, an aged Zhang Zhongjing reflects on his journey. Though his political title is forgotten by history, his medical text survives, laying the foundational canon for Traditional Chinese Medicine (Shanghan Lun and Jin Gui Yao Lue).
  - The film closes on an inspiring montage bridging history to the present: showing centuries of doctors saving lives using his formulas, and families gathering on cold winter days to share steaming bowls of dumplings born from one physician's enduring warmth and compassion.
