TITLE: THE MAN WHO SHAPED TOMORROW

LOGLINE:
Arriving in New York with fifty dollars and a sketchbook after World War I, charismatic French immigrant Raymond Loewy battles corporate cynicism during the Great Depression to revolutionize everyday consumer products, proving that beauty, utility, and elegance belong to the masses.

PLOT OUTLINE:

ACT I: THE FIFTY-DOLLAR IMMIGRANT
- Establishing Scene: 1919. Raymond Loewy, a decorated French Army officer wounded in WWI, arrives at Ellis Island with only $50 in his pocket. He starts work in New York as a commercial illustrator, but grows frustrated by the ugly, clumsy, industrial world around him—where everyday products are built like heavy factory machinery without regard for human touch or aesthetics.
- The Turning Point / Inciting Incident: As the Great Depression hits in 1929, American manufacturing collapses. Loewy boldly pitches corporate leaders with a radical premise: consumers aren't buying because products are visually repulsive and functionally outdated. Design isn't a luxury; it's an economic lifeline.
- Plot Point One: Sears, Roebuck & Co. takes a high-stakes gamble on Loewy, asking him to redesign their lagging Coldspot refrigerator. Loewy removes heavy iron legs, hides ugly cooling coils, adds rust-proof aluminum interiors, and creates an effortless latch system. The redesigned Coldspot becomes a runaway hit, tripling sales overnight and proving that good design can democratize luxury for working-class families.

ACT II: THE MAYA PRINCIPLE & STREAMLINE AMERICA
- Rising Action: Loewy founds his own design firm, pioneering the "MAYA" Principle—"Most Advanced Yet Acceptable"—the art of stretching product design into the future without shocking the public beyond what they can embrace. Loewy takes on the titans of American transit: redesigning Pennsylvania Railroad’s giant steam locomotives (PRR S1 and GG1) into sleek, futuristic streamliners that captivate a nation struggling through tough times.
- Midpoint: 1949. Loewy reaches the zenith of his fame, landing on the cover of Time magazine as "The Man Who Shaped America." From Coca-Cola fountain dispensers to Lucky Strike packaging, his touch is everywhere. However, success comes at a cost: intense corporate pushback against his forward-looking concepts, personal strain, and the relentless pressure to keep reimagining an evolving culture.
- Crisis / Plot Point Two: In 1961, iconic automaker Studebaker is on the brink of financial collapse. CEO Sherwood Egbert begs Loewy for a miracle—a radical, high-performance sports coupe designed in a matter of weeks to save the company. Loewy gathers a small team in a secluded Palm Springs house, working round-the-clock under intense pressure to invent something completely novel without a traditional chrome grille.

ACT III: AN ENDURING LEGACY
- Climax: Loewy unveils the Studebaker Avanti. It stuns the automotive world, breaking 29 world speed records at the Bonneville Salt Flats and becoming an instant design icon. Recognizing Loewy's unique ability to capture the spirit of progress, President John F. Kennedy personally enlists Loewy to design a dignified, inspiring identity for the nation's presidential aircraft—resulting in the timeless sea-blue, white, and chrome livery of Air Force One.
- Resolution: In his later years, Loewy reflects on his journey. From a penniless immigrant to the architect of modern daily life, he realizes his true achievement wasn't making luxury goods for the elite, but bringing form, function, and joy into the hands of millions of ordinary people every single day.
